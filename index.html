📖 The Golden Rules of Mortgage Underwriting Readiness

Pre-qualification from an online lender does not mean you are truly financially ready for 30 years of property ownership:

  • The 28/36 Qualifying Standard: Conforming Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans evaluate two critical ratios: your front-end housing expense ratio (Principal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance) should not exceed 28% of gross income, and total back-end debt should not exceed 36%.
  • Closing Costs and Prepaid Escrows: Many buyers save exactly their down payment and are blindsided by 2% to 4% in mandatory closing fees ($8,000 to $16,000 on a $400k home) covering appraisal, title insurance, loan origination, and property tax prepaids.
  • Post-Closing Reserve Cushion: Never drain your bank accounts to zero on closing day. Heating systems fail, roofs leak, and unexpected moves occur. Retaining at least 3 months of emergency PITI cushion protects against immediate foreclosure risk.

References: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) What is a Debt-to-Income Ratio; Fannie Mae Selling Guide Underwriting Standards.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 28/36 Debt-to-Income (DTI) rule?

Lenders prefer that your housing costs (PITI) do not exceed 28% of gross monthly income (front-end DTI), and total debt obligations including cars and loans do not exceed 36% (back-end DTI).

How much cash should I keep after closing?

Lenders and financial planners recommend retaining at least 2 to 3 months of full mortgage payments (PITI) in liquid cash reserves after paying the down payment and closing costs.

What credit score is required to buy a house?

Conventional mortgages typically require a minimum 620 credit score (740+ for prime rates). FHA loans allow scores as low as 580 with a 3.5% down payment.
